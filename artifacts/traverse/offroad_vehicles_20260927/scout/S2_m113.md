# S2: can Chrono's M113 tracked vehicle drive our f104 soil loop? (scouting, 2026-09-27)

Scope: scout S2 of the Polaris / M113 night. The question is whether the M113 can be put through the same soil
(CRM) collection and planner pipeline that the Gator went through (`arena_gator_20260925`), and at what cost.

What I did:
- I read Chrono's M113 model, its tracked-vehicle code and its tracked-vehicle-on-soil demo.
- I ran import-only checks on the cluster login node.
- I ran short local runs:
  - on flat rigid ground, each under 5 minutes;
  - 21 soil runs under `flock /tmp/luffy_crm.lock`, **about 9.2 minutes of soil wall time in total**.
- All scratch scripts and outputs are in `artifacts/traverse/offroad_vehicles_20260927/scratch/S2/`. No repository
  file was edited, no cluster job was submitted, and `NEDM_VEHICLE` was never set.

Anything not measured is marked **UNVERIFIED**.

Terms used below:
- **CRM**: Chrono's deformable soil, simulated as particles (SPH).
- **Soil-coupled body** (Chrono: "FSI body"): a rigid body that the soil pushes on.
- **Marker points** (Chrono: "BCE markers"): the points on a soil-coupled body's surface that the soil particles see.
- **Active domain**: the box around each soil-coupled body inside which soil particles are actually simulated. Soil
  outside every box is frozen.
- **Track shoe**: one link of the track.
- **Sprocket**: the toothed wheel that drives the track.
- **BDS**: Chrono's brake-differential steering driveline.

## 0. Bottom line

1. **It builds and drives, in both Chrono builds we would use.**
   - `veh.M113` and all 39 M113 classes import in the local source build, the local conda pychrono and both cluster
     builds.
   - Only the cluster `chrono-build-fsi` build has the soil module, which is the same situation as the HMMWV and the
     Gator.
   - Every call a tracked vehicle needs on soil is in the Python bindings of both the local build and
     `chrono-build-fsi`:
     - `GetNumTrackShoes`, `GetTrackShoe(side, i).GetShoeBody()` and `GetGroundContactGeometry()`;
     - `CRMTerrain.RegisterVehicle(ChVehicle)` and `AddRigidBody(body, ChBodyGeometry, check_embedded)`;
     - `GetNumBCE`, `GetFsiBodyForce` and `SetActiveDomainBody`.
   - The M113 source files are byte-identical locally and on the cluster (aggregate md5 `953ee4a5…`).
2. **Our route follower drives it sensibly, but only with the BDS driveline.**
   - With BDS, the steering command brakes the inner track. On flat rigid ground:
     - steering 0.25 gives curvature 0.13 to 0.19 /m;
     - steering 0.5 or more gives 0.39 to 0.55 /m (radius 2.5 to 3 m).
   - The frozen follower completes the Gator scout's test route at 2, 4 and 6 m/s. Its root-mean-square
     cross-track error is 0.91 to 1.05 m, against 0.49 to 0.63 m for the HMMWV and 0.64 to 0.74 m for the Gator.
   - The default SIMPLE driveline turns only by cutting torque to the inner track. At full steering it gives only
     0.10 /m, so it **cannot follow the route curvature limit of 0.125 /m**.
3. **It needs a 0.5 ms physics step, half the soil production step.**
   - With the collectors' SMC contact, the M113 fails on rigid ground:
     - at 2 ms the state becomes NaN at the first driven step;
     - at 1 ms it becomes non-physical, with the chassis 4.6 m up and moving at 8 m/s under full brake.
   - On soil at 1 ms the track jitters and the vehicle does not move under full throttle.
   - It is stable at 0.5 ms, which is also what Chrono's M113 demos use.
   - NSC contact is stable at 1 and 2 ms, but its multibody step is 2 to 3 times slower (section 2.4). It was not
     tried on soil.
4. **Cost on the real f104 arena: 7.7 times the HMMWV's wall time per simulated second, on the same GPU.**
   - Measured on the local RTX 5090, with 4.0 M soil particles and 4.0 M boundary marker points:
     - HMMWV: 2.35 wall s per simulated s;
     - M113: 18.1.
   - Per physics step, the soil part goes from 2.0 ms to 7.9 ms. The likely cause: 127 soil-coupled track shoes,
     and every particle is checked against every coupled body's active box at every step
     (`SphFluidDynamics.cu:200-207`, called every step at `ChFsiFluidSystemSPH.cpp:2044`).
   - The M113's multibody part (3.4 ms per 0.5 ms step, on the CPU) would, on its own, still cap it near 7 wall s per
     simulated s.
   - On the tiny test patch the ratio is only about 5 times, which hides the per-body cost.
5. **The soil traction of the only representation that works at our spacing is suspect.**
   - Chrono's demo keeps only track-shoe boxes larger than 0.32 m at our 0.08 m particle spacing. No M113 box
     qualifies, so the demo recipe **gives the tracks no soil contact at all**.
   - I therefore modelled each shoe as one flat pad box, 0.154 x 0.38 m, one or three marker layers thick.
   - On flat soil, the pads carry the full weight (105 to 115 kN against 111 kN) and the M113 reaches 4.0 m/s
     after 3.4 s at full throttle.
   - On soil tilted by 10°, 20° and 25° (slope made by tilting gravity), **the braked M113 slides backwards at 0.7
     to 1.1 m/s**. At 20° it keeps sliding even at full throttle.
   - On the same tilted soil, the Gator and the HMMWV hold still when braked, though neither climbs 20° from rest.
   - The pads have no grousers (the ridges that bite into the ground), and 0.08 m spacing cannot resolve shoe-scale
     features.
   - **A "better than the Gator" smoke test would therefore partly test this representation, not the vehicle.**
6. **Verdict.**
   - **Collecting the 15,235 soil ids with the M113 is not feasible tonight.**
     - Cost: about 140 simulated hours x about 15 to 24 wall s per simulated s on MI350X / MI210, i.e. about 2,000
       to 3,500 GPU-hours.
     - That is 40 to 70 wall hours on the ~48 GPUs idle at 23:15. At the 09-25 billing it is roughly 250 to 450 of
       the 742 node-hours left.
   - What is feasible tonight: a paired M113-versus-Gator soil smoke on the Gator pilot routes (144 drives, about
     20 to 30 GPU-hours, 1 to 2 wall hours on 16 to 24 GPUs), after a 30-minute local shoe-geometry check. Plus the
     code in section 6: two new files, no edits to existing ones.

### 1.3 How steering works for a tracked vehicle in Chrono

- **Sign convention.** `DriverInputs.m_steering` in [-1, 1], + = left, as for the wheeled vehicles. Measured: steering
  +0.5 gives yaw rate +0.89 rad/s.
- **What the vehicle does with the command.** `ChTrackedVehicle::Synchronize` (`ChTrackedVehicle.cpp:63-99`) first
  asks the driveline to combine the steering and brake inputs into a left and a right brake, then applies the engine
  torque through the driveline.
- **BDS** (`ChTrackDrivelineBDS.cpp:110-120`):
  - an open differential between the two sprockets, lockable by a clutch;
  - `brake_left = braking + steering` when steering > 0, and symmetrically on the right.
  - So **steering = braking the inner track** with up to 10,000 N·m x |steering|.
  - Turning therefore costs a lot of speed. Holding 3 m/s by throttle, the M113 fell to 1.8 to 2.4 m/s in steady
    turns; holding 6 m/s, to 2.1 to 3.8 m/s.
- **SIMPLE** (`ChSimpleTrackDriveline.cpp:98-113`):
  - a limited-slip torque split (maximum bias 1 for the M113, `M113_SimpleDriveline.cpp:28`);
  - the inner sprocket torque is multiplied by (1 - |steering|), and there is no braking.
  - Measured curvature: 0.034, 0.041 and 0.10 /m at steering 0.25, 0.5 and 1.0.

**Measured turns** (`m113_rigid.py turn`: speed held by throttle, steering held 12 s, circle fitted to the last 8 s):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- steering 0.3 gave about 0.12 rad/s of yaw at 3.9 m/s, i.e. curvature about 0.03 /m;
- steering 0.8 gave about 0.45 rad/s at 2 to 3 m/s, i.e. about 0.2 /m, with speed falling from 3.5 to 1.4 m/s in
  2.5 s.

The mapping from steering to curvature is therefore strongly non-linear and depends on the ground.

## 2. Coupling tracks to the soil

### 2.1 How our collector couples wheels today

In `scripts/crm_collect.py` `build_crm` (lines 69-132):
- `terrain.RegisterVehicle(vehicle)` at `:80`. In Chrono this makes the soil step also advance the vehicle
  (`CRMTerrain.cpp:40-45,64-65`).
- One `ChBodyGeometry` holding the HMMWV tyre mesh (`:45`, `:111-113`) is added to every wheel spindle with
  `terrain.AddRigidBody(wheel.GetSpindle(), geometry, False)` (`:114-116`). These are 4 soil-coupled bodies with
  207 marker points each.
- Then the active box of 2 x 2 x 1 m (`:118`), the arena built from the BMP (`:128-130`), and `Initialize()` (`:131`).
- Wheel forces are read with `terrain.GetFsiBodyForce(spindle)` (`crm_tire_fields`, `:135-153`).
- The Gator wrapper swapped only the geometry passed to `AddRigidBody`, through a stand-in terrain object
  (`scripts/ag_vehicle.py:206-228`). It asserts that exactly 4 wheels on axles [0, 0, 1, 1] were swapped (`:226`).

### 2.2 What tracks need

- **Soil-coupled bodies:** every track shoe must be one: **127 bodies** (63 + 64). The sprocket, the idler and the
  road wheels are not coupled in Chrono's demo; they sit inside the track loop.
- **Chrono's demo** (`demo_VEH_CRMTerrain_TrackedVehicle.cpp`):
  - vehicle `M113_Vehicle_SinglePin.json`, NSC contact, simple engine with a simple automatic gearbox (`:410-436`);
  - 0.02 m spacing, 0.5 ms step, active box 0.3 x 0.4 x 0.4 m, variable step, Holmes boundary, no shifting
    (`:117-195`);
  - soil from particle files of a prepared S-lane (`:223`);
  - it calls `CreateFSITracks` (`:479-515`): it takes each shoe's `GetGroundContactGeometry()` boxes, keeps only
    boxes with length and width > 2 x (marker layers - 1) x spacing and thickness < that value, and adds one
    `AddRigidBody(shoe_body, geometry, false)` per shoe.
- **The demo filter fails at our spacing.**
  - At 0.08 m spacing the threshold is 0.32 m.
  - The M113 shoe's ground boxes are 0.11 x 0.19 x 0.03 m (centre pad) and 2 x 0.1315 x 0.0542 x 0.02 m (side pads)
    (`M113_TrackShoeSinglePin.cpp:84-110`).
  - **None passes (0 of 3, measured).** Run as in the demo at our spacing, the tracks would have no soil contact.
- **What I used instead.** One pad box per shoe, covering the full pitch and width, with its bottom face on the shoe's
  bottom face:
  - "thin": 0.154 x 0.38 x 0.03 m, which gives 3 x 6 x 1 = **18 marker points per shoe**;
  - "thick": 0.154 x 0.38 x 0.16 m, three layers, **54 per shoe**.

  The box marker generator fills small boxes completely (`ChFsiFluidSystemSPH.cpp:2264-2290`). Marker totals:

  | Representation | Marker points |
  |---|---|
  | M113, thin pads | 2,286 |
  | M113, thick pads | 6,858 |
  | HMMWV tyres | 828 |
  | Gator soil cylinders | 88 + 88 + 170 + 170 |

  Marker count is negligible next to the 4 M soil particles; **the number of bodies is what costs**, see 2.4.
- **Active domain.** The default box (2 x 2 x 1 m, full size, centred on each body, `ChFsiFluidSystemSPH.cpp:279-284`)
  applies to all 127 shoes.
  - Shoes on the top run (0.86 m up) activate no soil.
  - The union over the ground-run and wrap-around shoes is about 6.5 x 4.2 m, i.e. about 27 m², against about
    5.3 x 3.8 m = 20 m² for the HMMWV: **about 1.35 times as many active particles** (geometry estimate).
  - Shrinking the box to 1 x 1 x 0.6 m did not change the cost on the tiny patch, where the multibody part dominates.

  s. **UNVERIFIED:** the ratio may differ on AMD GPUs.
- **Slope runs cost more for every vehicle** (tilted gravity, flowing soil), cause not identified:
  - M113 at 10° / 20°: 18.9 / 17.9 to 24.7 wall s per simulated s;
  - Gator at 25°: 4.8 against 1.5 at 20°;
  - HMMWV at 25°: 2.7 against 1.6.

**Possible ways to cut the cost (none tested):**
1. Give each shoe an empty ("inverted") active box with `SetActiveDomainBody`, and add one coupled dummy body (a tiny
   sphere inside the hull) whose box covers the whole track footprint. The per-particle loop then skips 127 bodies
   cheaply (`SphFluidDynamics.cu:201-202`).
2. Patch Chrono (C++): test one overall bounding box before the per-body loop. This needs a rebuild of
   `chrono-build-fsi`, so not tonight.
3. NSC at 1 ms: this halves the soil steps, but the multibody step is about 12 wall s per simulated s (rigid
   measurement), so the gain is at most about 1.5x. Not tried on soil.

- On this soil none of the three climbs 20° from rest.
- The M113's flat pads transmit too little shear to hold even a braked vehicle on 10°. The weight component along the

- This reflects the representation (single-layer, grouser-free boxes 1 to 2 particle spacings in size), not the
  track physics: real tracks out-grip wheels on soft soil.
- **Until the pad representation is calibrated, an "M113 against Gator" soil comparison is not interpretable.**
- The start footprints on f104 have a fitted grade of up to 8.8° over the M113's footprint (see 4, "launch check"),
  so settle slides are also a launch risk.

## 7. Risks

1. **Cost:** 7.7 times the HMMWV per simulated second on the real arena. The full id set is not feasible tonight,
   and it is a large share of the remaining allocation.
2. **Pad representation:** the demo's shoe filter yields no contact at 0.08 m, and my flat pads slide on 10° to 25°
   soil when braked. Any M113-versus-Gator result depends on this unvalidated choice. Grousers need finer spacing than
   0.08 m (the demo uses 0.02 m, which means 8 times more particles in 2-D and 64 times in 3-D).
3. **Step size:** SMC at 1 ms is unstable. NSC at 1 ms was not tried on soil. 0.5 ms doubles the soil steps.
4. **Speed:** about 4 m/s top speed on soil and about 6 m/s on rigid ground. Turning drag is heavy (BDS brakes the
   inner track). The 6 m/s route profiles degrade to "full throttle".
5. **Reference point at the front sprocket:** goals count as reached about 2 m early relative to the vehicle centre.
   158 of 1,200 groups put the M113's rear on terrain the start gate never checked.
6. **Launch-check failures** on sloped starts from the settle slide.
7. **Label shift** toward "unsafe by backward motion".
8. **Numerical noise** in the substitute spindle-speed columns (sprocket chatter at rest): harmless for the
   `--cond none` planner.
9. **Heading drift** at zero steering (0.57 rad per 10 s on rigid): the follower corrects it, but it adds steering
   activity.
10. **Cluster-versus-local ratio unmeasured**; per-node non-reproducibility applies as for the Gator (run paired arms
    in one job).
