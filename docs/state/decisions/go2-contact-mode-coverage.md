# Contact-mode coverage in the merged Go2 dataset

**Measured 2026-09-05, before any collection was specified.** 900 admissible episodes
from the merged flat set, 3,389,605 transitions. Mode is the 4-bit foot-contact state
from `foot_{fl,fr,rl,rr}_in_contact` (bit order fl=1, fr=2, rl=4, rr=8).

## All admissible episodes

| mode | feet down | count | fraction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | all four | 1,637,296 | 48.30% |
| 9 | fl+rr | 361,411 | 10.66% ← **diagonal** |
| 7 | fl+fr+rl | 312,637 | 9.22% |
| 6 | fr+rl | 293,269 | 8.65% ← **diagonal** |
| 0 | none | 253,712 | 7.49% |
| 11 | fl+fr+rr | 198,615 | 5.86% |
| 14 | fr+rl+rr | 129,668 | 3.83% |
| 13 | fl+rl+rr | 112,161 | 3.31% |
| **3** | **fl+fr — FRONT pair** | 30,801 | **0.91%** |
| 1 | fl | 17,385 | 0.51% |
| 2 | fr | 16,014 | 0.47% |
| **5** | **fl+rl — LEFT pair** | 12,963 | **0.38%** |
| 4 | rl | 6,059 | 0.18% |
| **10** | **fr+rr — RIGHT pair** | 2,945 | **0.09%** |

**8 of 16 modes fall below 1%.** Absolute counts matter as much as fractions: four of
those eight still exceed 12,000 samples. **The genuinely thin ones are modes 12 (1,991),
8 (2,678), 10 (2,945) and 4 (6,059).**

## The pattern is exactly what a trot predicts

Both **diagonal** pairs — 6 and 9 — carry 293k and 361k transitions. **All four lateral
pairs are under 1%**: front (3), left (5), right (10) and rear (12), with rear and right
the thinnest in the set. A trotting gait never places weight on a lateral pair, so those
configurations are essentially absent from a dataset generated by one gait.

## Falls do NOT supply the missing modes

The hypothesis was that 435 fall-containing episodes might already carry the diversity,
since a falling or recovering robot visits configurations a trot never does. **Measured
on 283,566 transitions from episodes containing a fall:**

```
  mode 0 (no feet down)   252,368   89.00%
  everything else          31,198   11.00%,  none above 4.7%
```

**A falling robot is airborne or collapsed, not standing on unusual foot pairs.** Falls
contribute almost entirely to mode 0 — already at 7.5% overall — and add essentially
nothing to modes 3, 5, 10 or 12.

## What would excite the missing modes

Lateral pairs carry weight when the robot is pushed **sideways** or is **rolling**. Rear
pair (12) means the front is unloaded — a forward pitch or nose-down push. Right pair
(10) means rolling left. **So lateral and roll-axis perturbations are the tool, not
stronger forward pushes.** The collector records `perturb_torque_x/y/z`; whether those
were ever non-zero in this dataset is worth confirming before specifying a collection,
since a roll-axis torque is the most direct excitation for modes 5 and 10.

## Scope

900 of 3,503 episodes, taken in index order, 3.39M of ~10.5M transitions. The proportions
are unlikely to move materially on the full set but this is a sample, not a census.

## Why this matters for contact-conditioned dynamics

The surrogate fits a function with repeated discontinuities — four feet making and
breaking contact several times per gait cycle. A single smooth transition model can only
average across them, which is consistent with the magnitude-compressed but
directionally-correct action response measured in the gate. Conditioning the transition
on a predicted contact mode turns one discontinuous function into a family of smooth
ones. **This histogram says the training data covers 8 of 16 modes well and 4 barely at
all**, and that the missing ones cannot be recovered from the existing fall data.
