# Fine-tune baseline arm: the measurement predicate

Written **before the merged baseline is computed**, so the definition is fixed in
advance rather than chosen once the numbers exist. Agreed between both collection
machines after two rounds in which we produced different answers from the same
words.

## What the baseline arm is

`go2_cts_150k.pt`, unmodified, in Chrono, driven by known commands — **which is
what the collection already is.** No new episodes, no reference trajectories, no
replay, no floor. Command tracking is `realised body velocity` against
`commanded body velocity`, and the collection contains both columns.

The trajectory-following alternative was rejected because it is degenerate: the
references were generated by this same checkpoint, so evaluating it against them
gives approximately zero error by construction and a fine-tuned policy could only
score worse.

## The predicate

    1. FAMILY SET stated explicitly in the output. Not implied by a directory
       glob, and not implied by a constancy test.
    2. Selection on the OBSERVED `cmd_vx_mps` column, not on
       `command_params`. The column is what the policy actually received.
    3. Command constant to 1e-6 over the SCORED WINDOW.
    3b. and that value must extend >= 5 s BEFORE the scored window, so a
       transient cannot be scored as steady-state tracking.
    4. 0.02 <= |cmd_vx| < 0.5 for the low band; sign separated, never pooled.
    5. >= 1500 rows. ratio = mean(vel_body_x over the last 1000 rows) / cmd_vx.
    6. Report n PER FAMILY beside every pooled figure.

## Why each clause exists — every one of them cost something

**1** — the two halves disagreed by 26 points on the pooled low-band median and
neither predicate named its family set. One side selected the `constant` family;
the other selected episodes whose command *happened* to be constant, admitting
`arc`, `yaw_step` and `weave`. Those track backward roughly twice as well, and
their weight in the pool set the pooled number. **The disagreement was entirely
composition and neither of us could see it, because neither predicate said what
was in the pool.**

**2** — selecting on `command_params` admits step families whose realised mean
includes stopped phases. Selecting on the observed column does not. The two
choices moved the pooled median in opposite directions.

**3 / 3b** — "constant over the scored window" and "constant over the whole
episode" produced different sets from the same sentence. Scored-window is the
tracking question and uses more data; **3b** is what makes it safe, since
scored-window constancy alone does not guarantee the robot has settled. Measured
on both halves: minimum margin 5.85 s and 12.44 s, **zero exclusions on either**.
So 3b is free here and verified rather than assumed — it will do real work on a
collection whose schedules switch later.

**4** — the lower bound is not optional. A realisation ratio with a 0.01 m/s
denominator is undefined regardless of which way the answer falls, and that is
true before anyone looks at the result, which is what makes the exclusion
principled rather than convenient. Four such episodes moved a backward-low median
from **+50.4% to −1.6%** and an acceptance criterion was nearly built on the
result. **Sixth appearance of the denominator problem in one day.**

**5** — steady state, because pooled-over-time figures answer a different
question: the same cell reads 55.1% pooled with transients and 32.8% at steady
state. Neither is wrong; they are not the same measurement.

**6** — this clause alone would have caught the 26-point disagreement on sight.
It is the same rule the level-3 report already applies when its pooled line names
its family mix, and it was not applied here.

## Agreement achieved under the predicate

Per family, backward, `0.02 <= |cmd| < 0.5`, steady state:

| family | sbel-pc | n | dorm-pc | n |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| arc | 58.9% | 26 | 60.8% | 25 |
| yaw_step | 67.2% | 23 | 60.4% | 21 |
| weave | 33.2% | 19 | 27.4% | 20 |
| constant | 29.8% | 23 | 24.6% | 33 |
| vel_step | 28.6% | 23 | 32.0% | 23 |

Within 7 points on every family. **The differences are NOT systematic** — 3 of 5
one way, 2 of 5 the other, mean −2.5 points, exact two-sided sign test p = 1.000.
The halves are consistent and there is no between-box effect to explain. That was
asserted twice as a real difference and withdrawn on measurement.

## The near-zero cell is a different measurement, not a smaller one

`|cmd| < 0.02` is not "very slow tracking", it is **stand still** — a task with no
denominator, scored on drift. It is reported separately and never pooled with
tracking.

And it splits again: **199 of 210 hold-still segments come from `stop_and_go`
alone** — a stopped phase mid-task — against a handful of genuinely zero-commanded
episodes. "Hold station mid-task" and "stand still for 40 s" are different tasks
before you ask how well the robot does either, so they get two names and two
numbers.

Corrected magnitude, contiguous segments of >= 5 s only: median net displacement
0.277 m over 5.5 s = **0.049 m/s**, net/path ratio 0.67, 15% swaying in place and
67% genuinely travelling. An earlier figure of 10.3 m came from differencing
positions across non-contiguous masked rows, so the displacement spanned the
intervals that had been excluded.

## Status

Predicate agreed both sides. Merged baseline unblocked. **The acceptance criterion
is deliberately still unwritten** — three of the four numbers used to size this
target so far were artifacts of how the statistic was formed, and the criterion
gets written against the merged baseline once, rather than fixed a fourth time.
